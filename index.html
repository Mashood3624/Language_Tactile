Abstract

Robots need touch to manipulate objects safely and reliably, as many properties such as softness, texture, and contact stability are hard to infer from vision alone. Vision-based tactile sensors produce different observations for the same material due to variations in optics, elastomer properties, and illumination, which hurts cross-sensor generalization.

We propose a language-guided distillation framework that learns sensor-robust tactile representations. Language encodes high-level semantic properties of touch (e.g., rough, soft, slippery) that remain invariant across sensing hardware, providing a sensor-agnostic supervisory signal.

Our method achieves 95% accuracy in the 100-shot setting, improves cross-sensor transfer by +13.3% on average, and yields up to +19% accuracy gains across six existing tactile datasets.

Title: Language-Guided Representation Learning for Robust Cross-Sensor Tactile Perception

Authors: Mashood M. Mohsan, Muhayy Ud Din, Binzhao Xu, Ahmad Abubakar, Irfan Hussain*

Affiliation: Khalifa University Center for Autonomous Robotic Systems (KUCARS), Khalifa University, UAE

Introduction

Tactile perception plays a fundamental role in robotic manipulation. While vision provides global scene understanding, touch enables inference of material properties and contact dynamics that are difficult to capture visually. Prior work shows that tactile sensing improves grasp prediction and material recognition.

Despite this progress, tactile perception remains strongly sensor-dependent. Differences in optical configuration, elastomer mechanics, illumination, and fabrication cause the same material to produce distinct contact images across devices. As a result, models trained on one sensor often degrade when applied to another, because learned features entangle semantic material properties with sensor-specific artefacts.

Humans describe tactile experiences using language that abstracts away sensing mechanisms. Words such as smooth, rough, soft, and slippery encode semantic material properties independent of hardware. Language therefore provides a sensor-agnostic semantic space for aligning tactile observations across sensors.

Methodology

The proposed framework uses a frozen language encoder to provide semantic supervision for a tactile Vision Transformer. The tactile encoder is trained with a combined loss: supervised material classification plus feature-level language distillation.

  • Language teacher encoder (BART) remains frozen during training.
  • Tactile encoder learns to align its features with language embeddings.
  • After distillation, the tactile encoder is frozen and only a lightweight classifier is fine-tuned for downstream material recognition.

Dataset

The paper constructs a multimodal tactile dataset with 56K samples by combining HCT and SSVTP data, adding human-annotated material labels. The dataset integrates tactile images, RGB views, language descriptions, and 32 material classes for benchmark evaluation.

Results

The proposed model demonstrates strong few-shot learning and cross-sensor generalization. It significantly outperforms tactile-only baselines and achieves nearly human-level material recognition accuracy across multiple benchmarks.

Few-Shot Learning

Reaches 95% accuracy in the 100-shot setting on the proposed tactile benchmark.

Cross-Sensor Transfer

Improves cross-sensor transfer by an average of +13.3% accuracy and up to +19% accuracy gains across six existing tactile datasets.

Benchmark Comparison

Outperforms baselines such as CMC, DeiT, and ConvNeXt on challenging tactile datasets including TAG, YCB, FEEL, SSVTP, HCT, and the proposed dataset.
