Language-Guided Representation Learning for Robust Cross-Sensor Material Recognition

A language-guided distillation framework for sensor-robust tactile representations that generalize across vision-based tactile sensors.

Abstract

Robots need touch to manipulate objects safely and reliably, as many properties, such as softness, texture, and contact stability, are hard to infer from vision alone. However, vision-based tactile sensors yield different observations of the same material due to variations in optics, elastomer properties, and illumination, leading to poor generalization when trained on a single or multiple sensors.

We propose a language-guided distillation framework for learning sensor-robust tactile representations. Language encodes high-level semantic properties of touch (e.g., rough, soft, slippery) that remain invariant across sensing hardware, providing a natural sensor-agnostic supervisory signal.

We construct a 56K-sample touch–language dataset with human-annotated material labels and train a tactile encoder to align sensor-specific tactile images with language embeddings in a shared semantic space. We evaluate our approach for few-shot learning and cross-sensor transfer and benchmark it on six existing tactile datasets.

Introduction

Tactile perception plays a fundamental role in robotic manipulation. While vision provides global scene understanding, touch enables inference of material properties and contact dynamics that are difficult to capture visually. Prior work shows that tactile sensing improves grasp prediction and material recognition.

Despite recent progress, tactile perception remains strongly sensor-dependent. Differences in optical configuration, elastomer mechanics, illumination, and fabrication cause the same material to produce distinct contact images across devices. As a result, models trained on one sensor often degrade when applied to another, because learned features entangle semantic material properties with sensor-specific artefacts.

Humans describe tactile experiences using language that abstracts away sensing mechanisms. Words such as smooth, rough, soft, and slippery encode semantic material properties independent of hardware. Language therefore provides a sensor-agnostic semantic space for aligning tactile observations across sensors.

Methodology

We train a tactile Vision Transformer using both supervised material classification and language-based feature distillation. A frozen language encoder serves as the teacher, while the tactile encoder learns to align its representations with language semantics.

  • Language teacher encoder (BART) remains frozen during training.
  • The tactile encoder aligns sensor-dependent tactile features with a language semantic space.
  • After distillation, the tactile encoder is frozen and only a lightweight classifier is fine-tuned for downstream tasks.

Dataset

The dataset integrates tactile images, RGB observations, natural language descriptions, and material labels. It contains 56K samples across 32 material classes, constructed from HCT and SSVTP sources with human-annotated class labels for supervised evaluation.

Results

Our approach yields strong improvements in few-shot learning and cross-sensor transfer. It outperforms tactile-only baselines and generalizes to multiple tactile datasets with very limited adaptation data.

Few-Shot Performance

Achieves 95% accuracy in the 100-shot setting on the proposed tactile benchmark.

Cross-Sensor Transfer

Improves cross-sensor transfer by an average of +13.3% accuracy and up to +19% accuracy on existing tactile datasets.

Benchmark Gains

Outperforms baselines such as CMC, DeiT, and ConvNeXt across TAG, YCB, FEEL, SSVTP, HCT, and the proposed dataset.
